1. Problem Statement & Proposed Solution

Addressing complex hydrological vulnerabilities in Punjab river basins

Punjab, the fertile agrarian heartland of northwestern India, is traversed by three major Himalayan rivers: the Sutlej, Beas, and Ravi. During the southwest monsoon season (July to September), peak precipitation in the upper Shivalik and Himachal Pradesh catchments leads to massive reservoir releases from the Bhakra Dam, Pong Dam, and Ranjit Sagar Dam into Punjab's river corridors.

Traditional flood monitoring relies heavily on isolated physical river gauge readings. This single-variable approach frequently fails because localized floods in Punjab occur due to multiple distinct mechanisms:

  • Cloudburst Inundation: Extreme 24h rainfall overpowering soil absorption even when rivers are safe.
  • Urban Storm Drainage Choking: Industrial channels and choked municipal drains (e.g. Buddha Nullah in Ludhiana) causing catastrophic backflow.
  • River Embankment Overtopping: Dam releases and Himalayan surges overtopping dykes in low-lying confluence areas (e.g. Kapurthala Mand area).
  • Compound Catastrophes: Simultaneous heavy downpours, saturated soil, and river surges (e.g. July 2023 Punjab floods).

Proposed Solution: This platform implements a localized, explainable dual-engine machine learning system that couples multivariate real-time atmospheric telemetry with topographical features and drainage congestion indices to deliver predictive flood hazard ratings alongside cause-aware diagnostic classification and automated response priority planning.

2. System Architecture & Data Flow

Modular, resilient, and non-blocking pipeline

1. Telemetry Ingestion

Open-Meteo REST API

Fetches real-time temperature, humidity, precipitation, and soil moisture for coordinates with a 3s timeout. Falls back automatically to calibrated district seasonal baselines if offline.

2. Preprocessing & Imputation

StandardScaler Pipeline

Sanitizes raw telemetry, validates physical boundaries (soil moisture: 5–100%), and normalizes the 9-dimensional feature vector.

3. Dual ML Inference

Random Forest Classifiers

Executes parallel inference: flood_model.pkl (130 trees, 4 risk tiers) and cause_model.pkl (100 trees, 5 probable causes) with full class probabilities.

4. XAI & Response Planning

Explainability & Rules

Attributes feature contributions via Gini importance, performs sensitivity What-If analysis, and assigns emergency priorities and resource allocation directives.

5. Spatial UI & Audit

Leaflet.js & SQLite

Renders interactive OpenStreetMap with color-coded risk halos, updates charts with Chart.js, logs alerts, and stores records in SQLite.

3. Hydrological Modeling & Physics Formulation

USDA SCS Runoff Curve Number & Stage-Discharge formulations

The synthetic training instances are generated using an empirical hydrologic runoff formulation grounded in the USDA SCS Runoff Curve Number (CN) method and Central Water Commission (CWC) stage-discharge relations:

S = 0.35 × (R_24 × [M_soil / 100]) + 0.15 × (0.22 × R_7d) + 0.15 × (0.35 × [100 - C_drain]) + 0.25 × ΔH_river + 0.05 × E_topo + 0.05 × D_dist + ε

Where:

  • R_24 × (M_soil / 100): Infiltration excess surface runoff potential.
  • 0.22 × R_7d: Antecedent soil saturation diminishing ground absorption capacity.
  • (100 - C_drain): Drainage congestion penalty reflecting culvert choking and backflow.
  • ΔH_river: River surcharge pressure relative to local danger stage.
  • E_topo, D_dist: Topographical depression pooling and river channel proximity.
  • ε ~ N(0, 3.0): Sensor measurement stochastic noise.

5. Limitations & Future Scope

Scientific boundaries and technological expansion roadmap

Current System Limitations:

  • Ephemeral Container Storage: On Render free tier, SQLite database files reset upon container restarts. Enterprise deployments should attach a hosted PostgreSQL database.
  • Synthetic Hydrologic Training: While calibrated against authentic CWC/IMD historical monsoon flood thresholds, sensor training data uses USDA SCS simulation formulas due to restricted public access to raw continuous gauge telemetry.
  • Point-Based Forecasts: Open-Meteo forecasts reflect geographic centroids for district headquarters rather than hyper-localized micro-catchments.

Future Engineering Scope:

  • Satellite SAR Inundation Mapping: Integration of Sentinel-1 Synthetic Aperture Radar (SAR) imagery to map active water inundation extents during cloud cover.
  • IoT River Gauge Integration: Direct MQTT/LoRaWAN sensor feeds from ultrasonic river depth sensors on Sutlej and Beas bridges.
  • Digital Elevation Model (DEM) Hydrological Routing: Processing 30m SRTM DEM data to simulate overland flow velocity vectors.
